# Course Syllabus: Physical AI & Humanoid Robotics

## Course Information

**Course Title:** Physical AI & Humanoid Robotics: Embodied Intelligence in the Real World  
**Duration:** 13 Weeks  
**Level:** Advanced Undergraduate / Graduate  
**Prerequisites:** Basic programming, Linear Algebra, Calculus, introductory robotics concepts

## Course Description

This comprehensive course explores Physical AI - the integration of artificial intelligence with physical systems. Students will learn to design, implement, and deploy intelligent robotic systems that can perceive, reason, and act in real-world environments. The curriculum emphasizes Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems that enable robots to understand natural language commands, perceive their environment, and execute complex physical tasks.

Physical AI represents a paradigm shift from traditional digital AI to systems that interact with the physical world through embodiment. This course covers both the theoretical foundations and practical implementation of humanoid robotics systems that combine manipulation, locomotion, and cognitive capabilities.

## Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Understand Physical AI Principles**: Explain the fundamental concepts of embodied intelligence and the differences between digital and physical AI systems

2. **Implement VLA Systems**: Design and implement Vision-Language-Action systems that integrate perception, language understanding, and physical action

3. **Develop Humanoid Capabilities**: Create systems for both manipulation and locomotion in humanoid robots

4. **Integrate Multimodal Systems**: Combine vision, language, and action modalities into cohesive robotic systems

5. **Apply Modern AI Techniques**: Utilize large language models, computer vision, and machine learning for robotic applications

6. **Deploy Real-World Systems**: Bridge the gap between simulation and real-world deployment

7. **Evaluate Physical AI Systems**: Assess the performance, safety, and reliability of embodied AI systems

## Weekly Breakdown

### Week 1: Introduction to Physical AI
- Definition and scope of Physical AI
- Embodied vs. digital intelligence
- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems
- Course overview and expectations

### Week 2: ROS 2 Fundamentals
- ROS 2 architecture and concepts
- Nodes, topics, services, actions
- Message types and communication patterns
- Launch files and parameter management

### Week 3: URDF Modeling
- Robot description format
- Kinematic chains and joint definitions
- Visual and collision properties
- Multi-robot systems and transmissions

### Week 4: Simulation with Gazebo & Unity
- Physics simulation environments
- Sensor modeling and integration
- Gazebo simulation tools
- Unity robotics integration

### Week 5: NVIDIA Isaac
- Isaac Sim and robotics platform
- GPU-accelerated perception
- Isaac ROS packages
- Isaac Navigation and manipulation

### Week 6: SLAM & Navigation
- Simultaneous localization and mapping
- Navigation systems and path planning
- Costmap management
- Dynamic obstacle avoidance

### Week 7: Vision-Language-Action Systems
- Multimodal perception systems
- Vision-language models for robotics
- Action generation from multimodal input
- OpenVLA and related frameworks

### Week 8: Humanoid Locomotion
- Bipedal walking patterns
- Balance control and stability
- Inverse kinematics for locomotion
- Dynamic walking control

### Week 9: Manipulation
- Grasp planning and execution
- Dexterous manipulation
- Force control and tactile sensing
- Tool use and interaction

### Week 10: Conversational Robotics
- Natural language processing for robots
- Speech recognition and synthesis
- Dialogue management systems
- Human-robot interaction

### Week 11: LLM-based Planning
- Large language models for task planning
- Reasoning and problem solving
- Tool usage and function calling
- Cognitive architectures

### Week 12: Sim-to-Real & Deployment
- Domain randomization and transfer learning
- System identification and calibration
- Real-world deployment challenges
- Safety and reliability considerations

### Week 13: Capstone Project
- Integration of all course components
- Physical AI system implementation
- Demonstration and evaluation
- Future directions and research

## Assessment Methods

### Practical Assignments (60%)
- Weekly programming assignments implementing course concepts
- Simulation-based projects
- Code quality, documentation, and functionality

### Midterm Project (15%)
- Individual project implementing a Physical AI system component
- Written report and code submission
- Presentation of approach and results

### Final Capstone Project (20%)
- Comprehensive Physical AI system integrating multiple course concepts
- Team-based project (2-3 students)
- Demonstration, report, and presentation

### Participation & Engagement (5%)
- Class participation and discussion
- Peer reviews and collaboration
- Contribution to learning community
