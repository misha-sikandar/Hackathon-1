# Week 1 Exercises: Exploring Embodied Intelligence

This week's exercises are designed to help you understand the fundamental concepts of embodied intelligence and apply them to practical scenarios. These exercises combine theoretical reflection with hands-on exploration.

## Exercise 1: Embodied vs. Disembodied AI Comparison

### Objective
Compare and contrast embodied and disembodied AI approaches to a common problem.

### Instructions
1. Choose a common AI problem (e.g., navigation, object recognition, decision making)
2. Describe how a traditional (disembodied) AI approach would tackle this problem
3. Describe how an embodied AI approach would tackle the same problem
4. Compare the advantages and disadvantages of each approach
5. Discuss which approach would be more suitable for real-world deployment

### Deliverables
- A 500-word written comparison
- A diagram illustrating the differences in system architecture
- A brief discussion of which approach is more suitable for your intended application

### Rubric
- **Analysis Quality (40%)**: Depth and accuracy of comparison
- **System Understanding (30%)**: Correct understanding of embodied vs. disembodied approaches
- **Application Relevance (30%)**: Connection to real-world applications

## Exercise 2: Affordance Mapping Exercise

### Objective
Identify and map affordances in a familiar environment.

### Instructions
1. Choose a room in your home or workplace
2. List all objects in the room
3. For each object, identify its affordances (what actions it enables)
4. Classify affordances by type (support, manipulation, traversal, etc.)
5. Consider how affordances might change with different agents (human, robot, animal)

### Example
- **Chair**: Sit (human), climb (cat), support (place objects), hide under (small animal)
- **Table**: Support (objects), work surface, climb on (cat), hide under

### Deliverables
- A complete affordance map of your chosen room
- Classification of affordances by type
- Discussion of how affordances depend on the agent's capabilities
- A written reflection on how this relates to robot perception

### Rubric
- **Completeness (35%)**: Thorough identification of affordances
- **Classification (30%)**: Proper categorization of affordance types
- **Agent Perspective (20%)**: Understanding of agent-dependent affordances
- **Reflection Quality (15%)**: Insightful connection to robotics

## Exercise 3: Sensorimotor Contingency Experiment

### Objective
Experience sensorimotor contingencies firsthand and relate them to robot perception.

### Instructions
1. Perform the following experiment:
   - Cover one eye and move your uncovered eye left and right
   - Notice how nearby objects move more than distant objects
   - Now keep your eye fixed and move your head left and right
   - Notice the difference in apparent motion
2. Document your observations
3. Explain how this relates to depth perception in robots
4. Discuss how active exploration enhances perception

### Deliverables
- Detailed observations from the experiment
- Explanation of how this relates to robot stereo vision
- Discussion of active perception strategies
- Connection to the sensorimotor contingency theory

### Rubric
- **Observation Quality (40%)**: Detailed and accurate observations
- **Theoretical Connection (35%)**: Clear connection to sensorimotor contingency theory
- **Robot Application (25%)**: Understanding of application to robotics

## Exercise 4: Morphological Computation Analysis

### Objective
Analyze examples of morphological computation in nature and robotics.

### Instructions
1. Research three examples of morphological computation:
   - One from biology (animal/human)
   - One from robotics (existing robot)
   - One you propose for a future robot design
2. For each example, explain:
   - What physical property provides the computation
   - What computational task it performs
   - What advantage it provides
3. Draw parallels between the examples

### Deliverables
- Three detailed analyses of morphological computation
- Comparison of biological vs. engineered solutions
- Proposed design for a new morphological computation mechanism
- Discussion of the benefits of this approach

### Rubric
- **Example Selection (30%)**: Appropriate and diverse examples
- **Analysis Depth (40%)**: Thorough understanding of each mechanism
- **Original Design (20%)**: Creative and feasible proposal
- **Synthesis (10%)**: Connections between examples

## Exercise 5: Physical AI Simulation Conceptualization

### Objective
Design a simple simulation to explore embodied intelligence concepts.

### Instructions
1. Choose a simple embodied intelligence concept to simulate (e.g., active exploration, affordance learning, sensorimotor coupling)
2. Define the minimal environment for your simulation
3. Define the agent's embodiment (sensors and actuators)
4. Design the interaction loop between agent and environment
5. Describe what phenomena you hope to observe

### Example Concepts
- Active exploration for object recognition
- Affordance learning through interaction
- Sensorimotor contingency learning
- Morphological computation in locomotion

### Deliverables
- Simulation design document
- Environment description
- Agent embodiment specification
- Expected outcomes and learning objectives
- Sketch of the simulation architecture

### Rubric
- **Concept Choice (20%)**: Appropriate and interesting concept
- **Design Quality (35%)**: Well-thought-out simulation design
- **Embodiment Understanding (25%)**: Clear understanding of embodied principles
- **Expected Outcomes (20%)**: Clear articulation of learning objectives

## Optional Challenge: The Free Energy Principle in Robotics

### Objective
Explore how the free energy principle might apply to a simple robotic system.

### Instructions
1. Choose a simple robotic task (e.g., maintaining balance, following a wall, reaching a target)
2. Formulate this task in terms of prediction error minimization
3. Identify the internal states and sensory inputs
4. Propose how the robot might minimize prediction errors through action

### Deliverables
- Task formulation using free energy principle
- System identification (states, inputs, predictions)
- Action selection strategy
- Critical analysis of the approach

### Rubric
- **Formulation Quality (40%)**: Correct application of free energy principle
- **System Understanding (35%)**: Accurate identification of components
- **Critical Analysis (25%)**: Thoughtful evaluation of the approach

## Submission Guidelines

- Submit all written components in a single document
- Include diagrams where appropriate (hand-drawn or digital)
- Use clear, concise language appropriate for technical communication
- Cite any sources used in your research
- Aim for approximately 1500-2000 words total for all exercises

## Resources

- Recommended readings from the main lesson

- Time for the sensorimotor contingency experiment
- Access to online resources for research exercises

## Assessment Criteria

- **Understanding (40%)**: Demonstrate clear comprehension of embodied intelligence concepts
- **Application (35%)**: Successfully apply concepts to practical scenarios
- **Analysis (25%)**: Provide thoughtful analysis and critical thinking

Remember that these exercises are designed to deepen your understanding of embodied intelligence principles. Take time to reflect on how these concepts will inform your approach to building physical AI systems throughout the course.
